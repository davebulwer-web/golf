# Golf

A retro, pixel-art, isometric golf game in the style of Sid Meier's Sim Golf, built on the course generator from the Golf Manager game.

## Playing

Download `golf.html` and open it in a browser. It's a single self-contained file, so there's nothing to install.

The game opens on a title screen: start a **new career**, **continue** the one you have, or go to **free play**. A new career asks for your name and a player type (all-rounder, big hitter, athlete, weekender, beanpole or old hand, each tilting the starting skills a little) and a shirt colour. You start at a small club with £100, playing nine holes at a time against Bertie, at your home course or one of two away courses, each shown with its par, length and a difficulty rating out of five. The clubhouse between rounds shows your money, skills, the week of the season and your recent rounds. A season is twelve weeks. Each week has one midweek slot, for a practice session, a money match or a practice round, and then a weekend event, which you enter or skip; the weekend moves the week on. The career is saved in the browser as you go, after every hole, so you can close the page mid-round and carry on where you left off. `H` goes back to the clubhouse (or, in free play, the title). The career is built up step by step from the plan in the project; Besides a practice round, you can play a **money match**: nine holes of match play at home against one of six club members (Bertie, Long Larry, The Major, Swift Sal, Chippy Pete and your rival Vince Vallance), each with their own look, skills and nerve, for a stake of £10, £25 or £50. Each hole goes to the lower score, the match ends as soon as it's decided (e.g. 3&2), and the stake is won or lost (a halved match costs nothing). Your rival's skills keep two points ahead of yours. The clubhouse remembers your record against each member. Your skills move with your form: in a career round each of your shots that tests a skill is judged good or bad (a long drive on the fairway or one mishit or lost for power, a full shot near or far from the aim for accuracy, getting close or fluffing it inside 100 yards for short game, escaping trouble or staying in it for recovery, holing from 8 feet or more, missing from 4 or three-putting for putting), with a short note under the banner as it happens. At the end of the round each skill moves a quarter of a point per good shot over bad, at most a point either way, more slowly near the top and bottom of the scale. The clubhouse shows what changed and how many good and bad shots you played. In the midweek slot you can go to the **practice ground** and work on one skill with a five-ball drill on a hole of your home course, picked so the drill is fair (no water in the driving zone or guarding the green, and a greenside bunker for escapes): LONG DRIVE (power: in play and near your longest), TARGET GREENS (accuracy: approaches from 140 to 170 yards onto the green), CHIP TO THE CIRCLE (short game: pitches from 30 to 70 yards to within 3 yards), ESCAPE ARTIST (recovery: out of greenside sand and rough onto the green) and PUTTING CHALLENGE (putting: holing from 6 to 30 feet). The view zooms in for the short drills. A session always helps a little, from 0.2 of a point for no good balls to 1.2 for five, less near the top of the scale, and uses the week's midweek slot without moving the week on. The **pro shop** sells kit with your winnings, one piece for each slot (driver, irons, wedge, putter and balls). Each piece adds a point or two to one or two skills, shown in blue on the clubhouse skill bars, and wears out after a set number of rounds (ten for clubs, three for a sleeve of balls); the clubhouse says when something has worn out. Buying a piece you already have gives you a fresh one. Better kit unlocks at each tier up the ladder. Every weekend has an **event**. At the club it's the Saturday roll-up (nine holes, £2 to enter, £12 / £6 / £3 for the top three), the monthly medal in weeks 3, 6 and 9 (nine holes, £5, £40 / £20 / £10) and the club championship in week 12 (eighteen holes, £10, £100 / £50 / £25, and the title of club champion). They're stroke play at your home course against a field of thirteen: the six members you can play money matches against and six more regulars. You play with one of them, drawn at random, and everyone else's scores are worked out from their skills hole by hole (`src/events.js`), about a sixth of a shot per nine holes for each skill point, measured by playing the computer golfer at different skills. A leaderboard shows the top five and you as you go, ties are settled on countback, and the full results come up at the end. The clubhouse shows this weekend's event with ENTER and SKIP; a skipped event is still played, without you. Each event gives **order of merit** points to the top ten (25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 1), double for the week 12 event, and the clubhouse shows where you stand (TABLE shows the lot). At the end of the season the top three on the order of merit, or the winner of the week 12 event, go up a level, and your rival Vince comes too. **County level** plays eighteen-hole opens (£20, £150 / £80 / £40) at a different county course each week, ending with the county championship (£30, £400 / £200 / £100) at the hardest, against twelve county amateurs (Lofty Lewis, Sandy Banks, Rory Rough, Max Yardage, Penny Pitchmark, Barry Bunker, Ivy Irons, Colonel Cleek, Big Ron Rollins, Fairway Fran, Kenny Kickback and Hattie Hazard) and Vince. Practice rounds can then be played on the county courses too, and county kit opens in the pro shop. You keep your home club for practice and money matches. Above county come the **tours**. The **challenge tour** plays three-round events (£50, £1,000 to the winner, ten places paid) at its courses, with a cut after two rounds to the top twelve and ties, against twenty young pros (Benny Backspin, Lulu Lipout, Percy Pinhigh and friends), Vince and a second rival, Dex Dashwood, who keeps three points ahead of you; the season ends with the four-round Challenge Tour Final. The **main tour** plays four-round classics (£200, £10,000 to the winner, fifteen places paid, cut to the top sixteen) against thirty tour pros, ending with the Tour Championship. In a tour event the clubhouse shows where you stand, and each round can be played (PLAY ROUND) or worked out from your skills (SIM), which wears your kit but doesn't move your skills. A missed cut ends the event for you. The pros wear the sharper looks (athlete, beanpole, all-rounder) in tour colours. Events are dressed for the occasion by level: county events get banners, flags and ropes; the challenge tour adds stands round the closing greens, leaderboards, tents and the clubhouse set; the main tour has the lot, TV and all. Earning a place in the majors is recorded, ready for when they open.

Pick a shot from the bar at the bottom (or press `1` to `5`): normal, fade, draw, lob or runner. Then press on the course and drag to where you want the ball to land, and let go to set the aim. The dotted line shows the shot's flight and the oval shows where it's likely to land: it grows and turns red as you push for more distance. You can still change the shot type or drag again; press **HIT** (or space) to play it, or **CANCEL** (`Esc` or right-click) to clear it. Clubs are picked for you. Wind isn't in the preview, so aim off for it.

The game fills the window and shows the whole hole. Zoom in with the mouse wheel or `+` / `-`; zoomed in, the view follows the ball. Drag with the right (or middle) mouse button, or use the arrow keys, to scroll round the rest of the course, as far as its edge; `C` (or the next shot) brings the view back. Zoom out past the whole hole, or press MAP (`M`), for the whole course on one screen; click it or press `M` to go back. Every hole's number is shown over its flag and by its back tee, gold for the hole being played. The course round the hole is drawn a piece at a time as it scrolls into view (`piece` in `src/main.js`), using the same drawing as the hole itself, so the pieces meet without a seam. The two buttons under the wind panel (or `Q` / `E`) turn the view a quarter turn round the hole, as in Sim Golf, so you can see past trees and hills from another side. The course picture is drawn once, so a golfer standing behind a tree or a piece of furniture gets the scenery in front of them drawn again over the top (`sceneryInFront` in `src/main.js`): they're hidden behind it, and turning the view brings them out.

The whole course is laid out on one map, as in Sim Golf: each tee is a short walk from the green before, and the neighbouring holes show around the one you're playing, a little darker. On links and tropical courses the seaside holes run along one coastline, with open sea beyond.

The ground rises and falls. Hillsides are shaded (lit from the top left), with banks where the ground steps down, and tees and greens sit up on their own level. Uphill shots play longer and downhill ones shorter, a yard for each yard of rise or fall; the shot bar shows it next to the aim (e.g. `UP 6`) and the reach allows for it. The ball runs on downhill, checks up uphill, falls away across a slope and can trickle back off a steep bank. Greens tilt and roll: putts break downhill and downhill putts run further past. Alpine courses are the hilliest, links the flattest, and lakes lie flat in hollows.

Water costs a shot and you drop back on dry land. Past the woods is out of bounds (stroke and distance), and that includes the other holes and the open sea. Once you're on the green, putting is a little game of its own, seen from above like the 16-bit golf games. The putt runs up the screen to the hole, the green is mown in stripes and shaded lighter where it's higher, and arrows drift downhill across it (faster where it's steeper). Click or drag on the green to aim (it starts aimed straight at the hole), then PUTT (or space) sets the power bar swinging and any click (or space) stops it. The red marker over the bar is the hole's distance on flat ground, and the bar's range steps up for longer putts (MAX 15 FT, 25 FT and so on); the bar shows how far uphill or downhill the hole is, in inches. The ball then really rolls: the slope pulls it downhill as it slows, it drops if it reaches the hole slowly enough, and a ball going too fast catches the lip and spins off. Your putting skill adds a little wobble to the line and the pace. Bertie reads putts by trying lines and paces with the same roll, misreading a little more from further away. The aim line doesn't show the break, so reading the slope is up to you.

You play against Bertie, a computer golfer. Whoever is farthest from the pin plays next, as in a real game. Bertie picks a shot by trying each shot type and a spread of landing spots in his head, using the same shot model and wind as you, and takes the one that should leave the fewest strokes; his nerve decides how much a risky shot puts him off. You'll see his aim line for a moment before he hits.

Scoring is medal play. After each hole the scorecard shows the round so far for both of you (front nine, back nine, total against par); `Enter` goes on to the next hole and `Tab` shows or hides the card. The hole card at the top left keeps the running score.

Other keys: `N` / `P` jump to the next or previous hole (for testing; skipped holes stay blank on the card), `R` a new random course, `S` change course style. These are free play only. You can also pick a hole in the address bar, e.g. `golf.html?style=links&seed=7&hole=2`, which skips the title and goes straight to free play.

## Working on it

The game is plain JavaScript and a canvas, with no engine or libraries.

- `src/course-generator.js`: the course generator (see `src/COURSE-GENERATOR.md`).
- `src/career.js`: the career: your golfer, money, skills, week of the season and the round in progress, saved in the browser's storage.
- `src/practice.js`: the practice ground's drills: where each ball is played from, how it's judged and what a session is worth.
- `src/shop.js`: the pro shop's kit, tier by tier, and what the kit in your bag adds to your skills.
- `src/events.js`: the weekend events by tier, the fields, their simulated scores, multi-round events and the cut, the leaderboard, the order of merit and the end of the season.
- `src/form.js`: judging your shots for form, and moving your skills at the end of a career round.
- `src/course-list.js`: the career's courses for each tier (club, county, challenge tour, tour and majors), easiest first. It's written by `tools/curate-courses.mjs`, which builds candidate courses and rates each by having a computer golfer play it a few times (`tools/sim-round.mjs`), so harder tiers really do get harder. Run it again after changing the generator or the shot model.
- `src/menus.js`: the title, new career and clubhouse screens, drawn over a dimmed course.
- `src/course.js`: the game's tweaks to the generator: more doglegs and extra hazards, on their own random stream.
- `src/course-map.js`: lays the 18 holes out on one map (where each hole sits and which way it faces) and the coastline for seaside courses.
- `src/terrain.js`: hills and slopes: rolling ground over the whole course map, shaped on each hole (flat tees, tilted greens, smooth fairways, lakes in hollows).
- `src/shots.js`: the five shot types, reach (allowing for uphill and downhill), lies, club choice, scatter, wind, roll down slopes and water drops. Each lie (`LIES`) gives a range of a full shot's distance and widens the scatter: first cut of rough (within 4 yards of short grass) 85–95%, deep rough 60–75%, waste ground 75–90%, a fairway bunker 70–85%, a greenside bunker 45–60% and the trees 30–50% (a runner punched out gets a quarter more, up to 75%). Where in the range is down to how the ball sits, the same for the same spot, and the recovery skill wins back up to a third of what's lost. The shot bar shows the lie and the percentage you'll get. As in Sim Golf, some lies limit your shots: no runner from deep rough or a fairway bunker, normal or lob only from a greenside bunker, and runner or lob only from the trees; the others are greyed out.
- `src/golfers.js`: golfers and their skills.
- `src/ai.js`: how the computer golfer chooses its shot.
- `src/putting.js`: the ball rolling on the green (slope, friction, dropping in or lipping out), striking a putt with the power bar, and the computer's read of a putt.
- `src/green-view.js`: the top-down view of the green for putting, with its slope arrows, the hole, the aim and the balls.
- `src/play.js`: playing a hole and a round: strokes, putts, penalties and whose turn it is.
- `src/iso.js`: the isometric projection between grid tiles, yards (with the height of the ground) and screen pixels.
- `src/hole-view.js`: draws a whole hole as pixel art (ground, trees, tees, flag, golfers) with its neighbours around it.
- `src/art.js`: ground colours and textures and which trees grow for each course style, the sprites (trees and props from `src/tree-assets.js` and `src/prop-assets.js`, plus a placeholder golfer drawn in code) and a small bitmap font.
- `src/dinky.js`: the sprites: the golfer's poses (from `src/golfer-assets.js`, recoloured per player and mirrored to face the shot), the score and hazard pop-ups (from `src/popup-assets.js`), and the Dinky Tiny Golf lie banners.
- `src/weather.js`: the wind for a round and how it blows across each hole.
- `src/hud.js`, `src/main.js`: the on-screen panels and the page itself.

`index.html` loads the source files directly, which needs a local web server (for example `npx serve`). After changing anything in `src/`, rebuild the single-file version with `node tools/build.mjs`. `node tools/screenshots.cjs` saves screenshots of a few holes to `screenshots/`, and `node tools/playtest.cjs` plays holes for you aiming at the pin while Bertie plays his own shots (both need Playwright). `node tools/check-shots.mjs` prints tables of shot distances, scatter and hazard counts.

The golfers come from Dave's animation sheets, one per look, in `assets/golfers/<look>/source-sheet.png` (`club`, the golfer you play; `heavy`, Bertie; `athletic`; `casual`; `tall`; and `veteran`; the GOLFER button or G switches which one you play as). `python3 tools/cut-golfer.py` cuts out each frame (swing, putting, idle, celebrating, frustrated, shading the eyes, and on the club sheet walk, run and a few extras) at the same size and height with the feet in the same spot, into `assets/golfers/<look>/`, packed into `src/golfer-assets.js`. A new look needs its sheet's layout added to `SHEETS` in the script and its shirt colour to `SHIRTS` in `src/dinky.js`. The walk, run, pick-up and pointing frames aren't used yet. The score and hazard pop-ups (HOLE IN ONE to TRIPLE BOGEY, BUNKER, WATER HAZARD, OUT OF BOUNDS) come from Dave's pop-up sheet, `assets/popups/source-sheet.png`: `python3 tools/cut-popups.py` cuts and shrinks them with a dark outline into `assets/popups/`, packed into `src/popup-assets.js`. The flag, the ball in the air, the tee markers, and the cacti, gorse and heather come from Dave's props sheet, `assets/props/source-sheet.png`: `python3 tools/cut-props.py` cuts them out of its grass green background and shrinks them into `assets/props/`, packed into `src/prop-assets.js`. The rest of the sheet furnishes each hole (see `holeProps` in `src/hole-view.js`): a hole sign, bench, ball washer and bin behind the tees played from, 100 / 150 / 200 yard posts beside the fairway, a rake in the rough by about one bunker in three, and the clubhouse behind the 1st tee. The sign, bench, ball washer, bin, rake and clubhouse are drawn from four sides, and `propView` picks the one that matches the way each faces on screen as the view turns. For tournament rounds, the EVENT button (or `T`, or `&event=1` in the address) dresses the course with Dave's three tournament sheets, `assets/events/source-sheet.png`, `source-sheet-2.png` and `source-sheet-3.png`: `python3 tools/cut-events.py` cuts the grandstands, covered stands, TV towers, hospitality tents, scoreboards, presentation stage, lighting truss, marquee and entrance arch, and the leaderboards, trophy board, signposts, sponsor banners, ropes, crowd barriers, flags and feather flags, and the TV camera operators, camera platforms, camera crane, commentary desks, interview backdrop, broadcast tent, outside-broadcast vans, satellite dish, generator, flight cases and portable toilets into `assets/events/`, packed into `src/event-assets.js`. The sheets are drawn nearer front-on than the game's 2:1 isometric, so the script shears the buildings, boards and fences until their front edge runs at the game's slope and makes four diagonal views of each (`-sw`, `-se`, `-ne`, `-nw`), which `propView` picks so each faces its green. `tournamentSites` in `src/hole-view.js` places them for the whole course, spread out rather than piled up: stands behind most greens (always the 9th and 18th) and beside the tees of the par 3s, the 1st and the 10th; a TV tower and leaderboard by the 9th and 18th green (the scoreboard and stage on the 18th); the camera crane behind the 18th; the arch, a marquee, a tent, the trophy board, the interview backdrop and signposts round the clubhouse; and the odds and ends (a couple more TV towers, camera platforms, commentary desks, tents and leaderboards, one broadcast compound of vans, dish, generator and tent kept together, camera operators by a few greens, portable toilets behind a few tees, crowd barriers behind a few greens, sponsor banners on about half the holes, and rows of flags or feather flags by some tees) shared out by `eventExtras`, which gives each to the holes with least on them so a hole has a thing or two, never the lot. Each goes only where all the ground it covers is open (rough or woods) on every hole and clear of the other buildings, and trees are cleared round it. They're scenery only, and left off the whole-course map. Island greens get a wooden footbridge (`paintBridges` in `src/hole-view.js`): a straight run of tiles along the grid, so on the game's diagonals, from the green to whichever bank is nearest and open, preferring the side the generator's own `hole.bridge` heads for. It's only drawn: a ball landing on it is still in the water. The trees come from Dave's tree sheet, `assets/trees/source-sheet.png`. `python3 tools/cut-trees.py` cuts them out and shrinks them to game size (into `assets/trees/`), and `node tools/pack-assets.mjs` packs them into `src/tree-assets.js`. The ground textures come from Dave's texture sheet, `assets/ground/source-sheet.png`: `python3 tools/cut-ground.py` turns each swatch into a seamless 32 x 16 texture in `assets/ground/`, packed into `src/ground-assets.js`. Styles with their own ground colours (links, desert, heathland...) keep each texture's light and dark in their own colour. The font is still placeholder pixel art drawn from code.
