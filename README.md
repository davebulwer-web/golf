# Golf

A retro, pixel-art, isometric golf game in the style of Sid Meier's Sim Golf, built on the course generator from the Golf Manager game.

## Playing

Download `golf.html` and open it in a browser. It's a single self-contained file, so there's nothing to install.

Pick a shot from the bar at the bottom (or press `1` to `5`): normal, fade, draw, lob or runner. Then press on the course and drag to where you want the ball to land, and let go to set the aim. The dotted line shows the shot's flight and the oval shows where it's likely to land: it grows and turns red as you push for more distance. You can still change the shot type or drag again; press **HIT** (or space) to play it, or **CANCEL** (`Esc` or right-click) to clear it. Clubs are picked for you. Wind isn't in the preview, so aim off for it.

The game fills the window and shows the whole hole. Zoom in with the mouse wheel or `+` / `-`; zoomed in, the view follows the ball. Drag with the right (or middle) mouse button, or use the arrow keys, to scroll round the rest of the course, as far as its edge; `C` (or the next shot) brings the view back. Zoom out past the whole hole, or press MAP (`M`), for the whole course on one screen; click it or press `M` to go back. Every hole's number is shown over its flag and by its back tee, gold for the hole being played. The course round the hole is drawn a piece at a time as it scrolls into view (`piece` in `src/main.js`), using the same drawing as the hole itself, so the pieces meet without a seam. The two buttons under the wind panel (or `Q` / `E`) turn the view a quarter turn round the hole, as in Sim Golf, so you can see past trees and hills from another side.

The whole course is laid out on one map, as in Sim Golf: each tee is a short walk from the green before, and the neighbouring holes show around the one you're playing, a little darker. On links and tropical courses the seaside holes run along one coastline, with open sea beyond.

The ground rises and falls. Hillsides are shaded (lit from the top left), with banks where the ground steps down, and tees and greens sit up on their own level. Uphill shots play longer and downhill ones shorter, a yard for each yard of rise or fall; the shot bar shows it next to the aim (e.g. `UP 6`) and the reach allows for it. The ball runs on downhill, checks up uphill, falls away across a slope and can trickle back off a steep bank. Greens tilt and roll: putts break downhill and downhill putts run further past. Alpine courses are the hilliest, links the flattest, and lakes lie flat in hollows.

Water costs a shot and you drop back on dry land. Past the woods is out of bounds (stroke and distance), and that includes the other holes and the open sea. Once you're on the green, putting is a little game of its own, seen from above like the 16-bit golf games. The putt runs up the screen to the hole, the green is mown in stripes and shaded lighter where it's higher, and arrows drift downhill across it (faster where it's steeper). Click or drag on the green to aim (it starts aimed straight at the hole), then PUTT (or space) sets the power bar swinging and any click (or space) stops it. The red marker over the bar is the hole's distance on flat ground, and the bar's range steps up for longer putts (MAX 15 FT, 25 FT and so on); the bar shows how far uphill or downhill the hole is, in inches. The ball then really rolls: the slope pulls it downhill as it slows, it drops if it reaches the hole slowly enough, and a ball going too fast catches the lip and spins off. Your putting skill adds a little wobble to the line and the pace. Bertie reads putts by trying lines and paces with the same roll, misreading a little more from further away. The aim line doesn't show the break, so reading the slope is up to you.

You play against Bertie, a computer golfer. Whoever is farthest from the pin plays next, as in a real game. Bertie picks a shot by trying each shot type and a spread of landing spots in his head, using the same shot model and wind as you, and takes the one that should leave the fewest strokes; his nerve decides how much a risky shot puts him off. You'll see his aim line for a moment before he hits.

Scoring is medal play. After each hole the scorecard shows the round so far for both of you (front nine, back nine, total against par); `Enter` goes on to the next hole and `Tab` shows or hides the card. The hole card at the top left keeps the running score.

Other keys: `N` / `P` jump to the next or previous hole (for testing; skipped holes stay blank on the card), `R` a new random course, `S` change course style. You can also pick a hole in the address bar, e.g. `golf.html?style=links&seed=7&hole=2`.

## Working on it

The game is plain JavaScript and a canvas, with no engine or libraries.

- `src/course-generator.js`: the course generator (see `src/COURSE-GENERATOR.md`).
- `src/course.js`: the game's tweaks to the generator: more doglegs and extra hazards, on their own random stream.
- `src/course-map.js`: lays the 18 holes out on one map (where each hole sits and which way it faces) and the coastline for seaside courses.
- `src/terrain.js`: hills and slopes: rolling ground over the whole course map, shaped on each hole (flat tees, tilted greens, smooth fairways, lakes in hollows).
- `src/shots.js`: the five shot types, reach (allowing for uphill and downhill), club choice, scatter, wind, roll down slopes and water drops.
- `src/golfers.js`: golfers and their skills.
- `src/ai.js`: how the computer golfer chooses its shot.
- `src/putting.js`: the ball rolling on the green (slope, friction, dropping in or lipping out), striking a putt with the power bar, and the computer's read of a putt.
- `src/green-view.js`: the top-down view of the green for putting, with its slope arrows, the hole, the aim and the balls.
- `src/play.js`: playing a hole and a round: strokes, putts, penalties and whose turn it is.
- `src/iso.js`: the isometric projection between grid tiles, yards (with the height of the ground) and screen pixels.
- `src/hole-view.js`: draws a whole hole as pixel art (ground, trees, tees, flag, golfers) with its neighbours around it.
- `src/art.js`: ground colours and textures and which trees grow for each course style, the sprites (trees and props from `src/tree-assets.js` and `src/prop-assets.js`, plus a placeholder golfer drawn in code) and a small bitmap font.
- `src/dinky.js`: the sprites: the golfer's poses (from `src/golfer-assets.js`, recoloured per player and mirrored to face the shot), the score and hazard pop-ups (from `src/popup-assets.js`), and the Dinky Tiny Golf lie banners.
- `src/weather.js`: the wind for a round and how it blows across each hole.
- `src/hud.js`, `src/main.js`: the on-screen panels and the page itself.

`index.html` loads the source files directly, which needs a local web server (for example `npx serve`). After changing anything in `src/`, rebuild the single-file version with `node tools/build.mjs`. `node tools/screenshots.cjs` saves screenshots of a few holes to `screenshots/`, and `node tools/playtest.cjs` plays holes for you aiming at the pin while Bertie plays his own shots (both need Playwright). `node tools/check-shots.mjs` prints tables of shot distances, scatter and hazard counts.

The golfers come from Dave's animation sheets, one per look, in `assets/golfers/<look>/source-sheet.png` (`club`, the golfer you play; `heavy`, Bertie; `athletic`; `casual`; `tall`; and `veteran`; the GOLFER button or G switches which one you play as). `python3 tools/cut-golfer.py` cuts out each frame (swing, putting, idle, celebrating, frustrated, shading the eyes, and on the club sheet walk, run and a few extras) at the same size and height with the feet in the same spot, into `assets/golfers/<look>/`, packed into `src/golfer-assets.js`. A new look needs its sheet's layout added to `SHEETS` in the script and its shirt colour to `SHIRTS` in `src/dinky.js`. The walk, run, pick-up and pointing frames aren't used yet. The score and hazard pop-ups (HOLE IN ONE to TRIPLE BOGEY, BUNKER, WATER HAZARD, OUT OF BOUNDS) come from Dave's pop-up sheet, `assets/popups/source-sheet.png`: `python3 tools/cut-popups.py` cuts and shrinks them with a dark outline into `assets/popups/`, packed into `src/popup-assets.js`. The flag, the ball in the air, the tee markers, and the cacti, gorse and heather come from Dave's props sheet, `assets/props/source-sheet.png`: `python3 tools/cut-props.py` cuts them out of its grass green background and shrinks them into `assets/props/`, packed into `src/prop-assets.js`. The rest of the sheet furnishes each hole (see `holeProps` in `src/hole-view.js`): a hole sign, bench, ball washer and bin behind the tees played from, 100 / 150 / 200 yard posts beside the fairway, a rake in the rough by about one bunker in three, and the clubhouse behind the 1st tee. The sign, bench, ball washer, bin, rake and clubhouse are drawn from four sides, and `propView` picks the one that matches the way each faces on screen as the view turns. For tournament rounds, the EVENT button (or `T`, or `&event=1` in the address) dresses the course with Dave's three tournament sheets, `assets/events/source-sheet.png`, `source-sheet-2.png` and `source-sheet-3.png`: `python3 tools/cut-events.py` cuts the grandstands, covered stands, TV towers, hospitality tents, scoreboards, presentation stage, lighting truss, marquee and entrance arch, and the leaderboards, trophy board, signposts, sponsor banners, ropes, crowd barriers, flags and feather flags, and the TV camera operators, camera platforms, camera crane, commentary desks, interview backdrop, broadcast tent, outside-broadcast vans, satellite dish, generator, flight cases and portable toilets into `assets/events/`, packed into `src/event-assets.js`. `tournamentSites` in `src/hole-view.js` places them for the whole course, spread out rather than piled up: stands behind most greens (always the 9th and 18th) and beside the tees of the par 3s, the 1st and the 10th; a TV tower and leaderboard by the 9th and 18th green (the scoreboard and stage on the 18th); the camera crane behind the 18th; the arch, a marquee, a tent, the trophy board, the interview backdrop and signposts round the clubhouse; and the odds and ends (a couple more TV towers, camera platforms, commentary desks, tents and leaderboards, one broadcast compound of vans, dish, generator and tent kept together, camera operators by a few greens, portable toilets behind a few tees, crowd barriers behind a few greens, sponsor banners on about half the holes, and rows of flags or feather flags by some tees) shared out by `eventExtras`, which gives each to the holes with least on them so a hole has a thing or two, never the lot. Each goes only where all the ground it covers is open (rough or woods) on every hole and clear of the other buildings, and trees are cleared round it. They're scenery only, and left off the whole-course map. The trees come from Dave's tree sheet, `assets/trees/source-sheet.png`. `python3 tools/cut-trees.py` cuts them out and shrinks them to game size (into `assets/trees/`), and `node tools/pack-assets.mjs` packs them into `src/tree-assets.js`. The ground textures come from Dave's texture sheet, `assets/ground/source-sheet.png`: `python3 tools/cut-ground.py` turns each swatch into a seamless 32 x 16 texture in `assets/ground/`, packed into `src/ground-assets.js`. Styles with their own ground colours (links, desert, heathland...) keep each texture's light and dark in their own colour. The font is still placeholder pixel art drawn from code.
